I am a PhD candidate in Mechanical Engineering at UCLA, advised by Dennis Hong in the Robotics and Mechanisms Laboratory (RoMeLa), and an Amazon AI PhD Fellow. My research focuses on whole-body control for humanoid robots, combining learning-based and model-based methods for useful loco-manipulation scenarios, with a focus on the part that tends to be hardest: getting a learned policy off the simulator and onto real hardware that has to keep standing up.

I hold an M.S. from UCLA (2024) and a B.Sc. in Mechatronics Engineering from the Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería in Lima, Peru. I have been an Applied Scientist intern at Amazon Robotics, working on forceful loco-manipulation, and a humanoid whole-body control intern at IHMC, where I led hardware bring-up of the H1-2 control stack. I was an ETH RobotX Fellow in 2023, and before graduate school I worked on switched control of dynamical systems at UNICAMP in Brazil.

In my final years of undergrad and the time right after, I spent much of my time in my university’s space robotics groups, competing in several planetary rover competitions (and winning a few of them). I also organized events with the Space Exploration Project Group of the Space Generation Advisory Council, and in 2021 I served as an analog astronaut on a mission in the Mojave Desert. Designing machines for places people cannot easily reach is still the problem I find most interesting, and that is a big part of why I work on humanoids. The first builders on another world will not be human, but they may well look like us. Ad astra. 🚀

Away from the lab I write, I draw, and I read more science fiction than is strictly defensible.

Platforms

Robots I have worked on, drawn to true relative height — from a 270 g quadruped to a 1.78 m humanoid. On each one the work spans both simulation and deployment on the physical machine. Select a robot for detail.

0 m1 m1.8 m
  • MiNI-Q — RoMeLa, UCLA, 2026 (11 cm, 270 g). Simulation and hardware. Unified learned locomotion on a 270 g quadruped with eight continuous-rotation legs; a ~26k-parameter policy runs entirely onboard a dual-ESP32 system at 50 Hz. Submitted to ICRA 2027.
  • DARwIn-OP — RoMeLa, UCLA (46 cm). Simulation and hardware. RoMeLa’s open-platform humanoid, and the ancestor of the lab’s later machines.
  • High-Torque Humanoid — RoMeLa, UCLA, 2025–26 (90 cm). Simulation and hardware. Full-stack C++ ROS2 control on an in-development platform: hardware integration, multi-threaded state estimation, RL-based locomotion control and template-model-based adaptation for robust real-time walking.
  • Booster T1 — RoMeLa, UCLA, 2026 (1.18 m). Simulation and hardware. Reinforcement learning and software lead for RoboCup 2026, migrating ARTEMIS’s model-based ROS2 stack to a learning-based one with an end-to-end RL locomotion pipeline in Isaac Gym.
  • Unitree G1 — Amazon Robotics, 2026 (1.32 m). Simulation and hardware. Whole-body control for forceful loco-manipulation, combining model predictive control, control-barrier-function safety layers and learning-based methods.
  • ARTEMIS — RoMeLa, UCLA, 2023–24 (1.42 m). Simulation and hardware. Real-time computer vision and ball tracking for soccer. Winner of the Adult Size league at RoboCup 2024, and first place in the Humanoid Free Walk at IEEE Humanoids 2023.
  • Kid COSMO — RoMeLa, UCLA, 2024–25 (1.45 m). Simulation and hardware. C++17 upper-body control and a C++ ONNX sim-to-real pipeline for imitation-learning balance and walking. Mike Stilman Award, IEEE Humanoids 2025.
  • Unitree H1-2 — IHMC, 2025 (1.78 m). Simulation and hardware. Led hardware bring-up of the whole-body control stack and built a WBMPC robustness testing framework across the Nadia and H1-2 humanoids.
